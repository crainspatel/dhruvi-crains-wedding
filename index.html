HOW WE GOT HERE

Our Story

Three cities, a lot of little moments, and us.

01 / BOSTON

Chilly nights, hot chocolates.

It started on a chilly winter night in Boston. Two people, saat samundar paar, far from everything familiar, carrying equal parts excitement and nervousness about the life ahead. We happened to cross paths at a friend’s place, with absolutely no idea that a random evening would turn into a conversation neither of us would ever want to hang up on.

Soon, there were hot chocolates, cold coffees, ice cream dates, and hours spent talking about things that had never seemed particularly interesting before. Somewhere along the way, even the most ordinary days became entertaining. Of course, there were also arguments over the silliest things and board games taken far too seriously. Some things haven’t changed—we’re still two ridiculously competitive children at play.

02 / SEATTLE

Gloomy mornings, cozy days.

Then came Seattle, with its gloomy mornings and its own kind of warmth. We cooked together, showed up at the same office in our athleisure, and slowly built a life that felt wonderfully uncomplicated.

Seattle gave us room to grow, both in our careers and together. It also brought us friendships, laughter, and festivals celebrated like one big family. There was something about Apartment 1721 that made a city so far from home feel a lot closer to it.

03 / THE BAY AREA

Sunny afternoons, and us.

Eventually, the gloomy mornings gave way to the sunny afternoons of the Bay Area, where we’re now building another little corner of life together.

We’re still figuring each other out. Still getting annoyed over trivial things, still finding new reasons to laugh, and still making plans that somehow turn into impromptu trips. We’ve traveled to beautiful places and met wonderful people, but some of our favorite memories are simply the days when we had nothing much to do and nobody else around.

Because somewhere between Boston, Seattle, and the Bay Area, we discovered something rather simple:

One was less, but two were always more than enough.
